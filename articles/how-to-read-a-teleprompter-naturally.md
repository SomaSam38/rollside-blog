# How to read a teleprompter without looking like you are reading

Viewers can usually tell when someone is reading. The eyes drift, the voice flattens and the pace stays oddly even. Most of this comes down to setup and habit, not talent. Here is how to read a teleprompter naturally, from where you put the words to what to do when you fluff a line.

## Keep the words close to the lens

When your eyes sit noticeably off the lens, viewers feel it. The closer your words are to the lens, the more it looks like you are talking to them. There are two common approaches, and they work differently.

**A separate reader near your filming phone.** You film on one phone and display your script on a second phone or tablet placed just below it. Keep the reading area at the very top of the reader screen, so your next words sit as close to the lens as possible. This is simple and portable. Your eyeline will be slightly below the lens, so the closer the two devices sit, the better.

**A specialist mirror rig.** A reflector rig places angled glass in front of the camera lens. The script shows on a screen beneath it and reflects in the glass, so the words appear over the lens while the camera films through the glass. Because the reflection reverses the text, the display needs to show it mirrored. These rigs give a more direct eyeline but take more setting up. Test the placement and orientation with your actual equipment before you record.

Whichever you use, a little more distance between you and the camera tends to make small eye movements less obvious. Try a few positions and watch the playback.

## Make the layout easy to scan

A wide block of small text forces your eyes to sweep across the screen. A narrow column of larger text keeps them still. Compare the same line in two layouts:

```text
Today I'm going to show you three ways to make your kitchen lighting work better on camera.
```

```text
Today I'm going to show you
three ways to make
your kitchen lighting
work better on camera.
```

The second keeps your eyes in a small area. To get there:

- Narrow the reading width.
- Increase the text size until it is comfortable from your filming position.
- Choose strong contrast between text and background for the light in your room.
- Keep only a line or two in focus, so you always know where you are.

## Let the pace follow you

Racing the scroll is one of the quickest ways to sound like you are reading. If you use timed scrolling, start slower than feels natural and speed up only once you are comfortable. You need room to pause, breathe and look up.

If your prompter can follow your voice, it moves when you speak and waits when you stop, which makes deliberate pauses easier. Either way, rehearse at the speed you will film at, not a quicker "just checking" pace.

## Bring the expression back

Reading tends to strip out the rise and fall of normal speech. A few habits help put it back:

- **Know the script well enough to glance.** The prompter is a safety net, not something to decode word by word.
- **Picture one person.** Talk to a friend who asked you the question, not to "everyone watching".
- **Mark the moments that matter.** A note for a smile, a pause or a stressed word reminds you to vary your delivery.
- **Read slightly ahead.** Taking in the next phrase before you say it lets you deliver it as a thought, not a string of words.
- **Let yourself move.** If you would gesture in conversation, do it here too.

## Recover from mistakes without restarting

Everyone stumbles. What matters is how easy the take is to fix later.

1. **Small slip? Keep going.** A tiny wobble often sounds more human than a perfect read.
2. **Bigger mistake? Pause, then restart the sentence.** Stop, breathe and say the whole sentence again from the beginning. That gives you a clean cut point in the edit.
3. **Keep the camera rolling.** Stopping for every error breaks your rhythm. Fix it in the edit instead.
4. **Lost your place?** Look to the top of the reading area, find the line and pick up from the start of that sentence.

Note the lines that keep tripping you up. Rewording them usually works better than another ten takes.
