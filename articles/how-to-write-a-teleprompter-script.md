# How to write a teleprompter script that sounds like you

A teleprompter can help you sound clear and confident. It can also make you sound like you are reading the terms and conditions. The difference is rarely the device. It is the script. Most stiff scripts were written to be read silently, then spoken aloud.

## Start by talking, not typing

Before you open a blank document, say your idea out loud. Explain it as if a friend has just asked you about it. Record yourself with a voice memo app and play it back.

You will hear phrases, rhythms and examples you would never think to type. Transcribe the best parts. That rough transcript is a better first draft than anything written from scratch, because it already sounds like you.

Then answer three questions in one line each:

- Who is this video for?
- What is the one thing they should know or do by the end?
- What would you say first if they asked you in person?

If you cannot answer the second question in a sentence, the script will wander. Fix that before writing anything else.

## Write for the ear

A spoken sentence needs to be easy to follow on the first listen.

- **Keep sentences short.** One idea each.
- **Use contractions.** "You're" and "don't" sound like speech. "You are" and "do not" sound like a letter.
- **Choose the words you actually say.** "Use", not "utilise". "Help", not "facilitate".
- **Add signposts.** "Here's the thing." "Step two." They help viewers keep up.
- **Write numbers as you say them.** "Ten to fifteen minutes" is easier to read aloud than "10-15 mins".

## A before and after example

Here is a script for a short Reel about batch filming, first written like an article.

**Before**

> Batch filming is a content production method whereby creators record multiple videos in a single session, thereby maximising efficiency and ensuring consistency. It is recommended that scripts are prepared in advance, that consistent attire is utilised, and that sufficient time is allocated for setup, which can otherwise represent a significant proportion of total production time.

Every word is correct. Almost nobody talks like that.

**After**

> Want to film a week of videos in one afternoon?
>
> That's batch filming. You set up once, then record several videos back to back.
>
> Here's how to make it work.
>
> Write all your scripts first. Not on the day. Before. [pause]
>
> Wear the same top, or bring a couple of changes.
>
> And give yourself time to set up. Setting up is the slow bit. Do it once, and every video after that is quicker.

What changed:

- It opens with a question the viewer might already be asking.
- Long sentences became short ones, one idea per line.
- Formal words such as "utilised" and "allocated" became everyday ones.
- Passive phrasing became "you", speaking to one person.
- A pause is marked where the point needs a moment to land.

## Edit for breath

Now read the new version aloud at your normal speaking pace. Wherever you run short of air or rush to finish, the line is too long.

- **One breath per line or short paragraph.** Line breaks become natural pauses when you read from a prompter.
- **Mark pauses on purpose.** A blank line or a note such as [pause] reminds you to stop rather than race on.
- **Emphasise sparingly.** One stressed word per section is plenty. Mark too many and nothing stands out.
- **Swap words you trip over.** If you stumble on "particularly" every time, use "especially" or cut it.
- **Cut warm-up lines.** "In this video I'm going to talk about..." usually adds nothing.

Keep a little of your natural style, though. If you genuinely say "right, so" before a new point, leave one in. That is part of what makes it sound like you.

## Rehearsal checklist

Before you film, run through this list:

1. Read the script aloud twice, away from the camera.
2. Mark every line you stumbled on and rewrite it.
3. Check that the first sentence gives the viewer a reason to keep watching.
4. Check that each line fits in one comfortable breath.
5. Load the script onto your prompter at the text size and speed you plan to use.
6. Do one full run without stopping, even if you make mistakes.
7. Record a rehearsal and listen back. Does it sound like you talking, or you reading?
8. Read the opening and closing lines once more. They set the tone and send people off.
