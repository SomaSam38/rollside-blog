# A simple talking head video setup for solo creators

When you film yourself, you are also the camera operator, the sound recordist and the lighting crew. That is a lot to juggle, so the best talking head video setup is one you can repeat without thinking.

This guide gives you a six-step process: message, frame, light, audio, takes and review. Follow it the same way each time and setting up will get quicker.

## 1. Message: decide what this video is for

Before you touch the camera, finish this sentence: "This video helps [who] to [do what]." Then decide:

- **Vertical or horizontal?** This shapes every framing decision that follows.
- **Will you add text or captions in the edit?** If so, leave space for them in the frame.
- **How many videos are you filming today?** If they share a setup, film them in one session.

## 2. Frame: set the shot once, then mark it

- **Put the lens at eye level.** A camera looking up or down at you changes how you come across. Raise the phone, not your chin.
- **Place your eyes roughly a third of the way down the frame.** Leave a little room above your head, not a large gap.
- **Move a step or two away from the wall.** This reduces shadows behind you and makes the background look less flat.
- **Tidy the background.** Look for anything that pulls attention, such as a bright lamp, a cluttered shelf or a plant that seems to grow out of your head.

Once the shot looks right, mark it. Put a small piece of tape on the floor where you sit or stand, note the height of your stand and take a quick photo of the whole setup. Use those notes to rebuild the shot next time.

## 3. Light: face the light

The simplest reliable approach is to face a window, or angle your main light towards your face from slightly to one side.

- **Avoid a bright window behind you.** It can leave your face too dark.
- **Be wary of overhead light on its own.** It can leave dark shadows under your eyes.
- **Watch for mixed light.** Daylight from one side and a warm lamp from the other can give your skin two different colours.
- **Remember daylight moves.** If you rely on a window, film at a similar time each session and check the shot between videos.

If your camera app lets you lock focus and exposure, do it once you are in position, so the image does not shift when you move your hands.

## 4. Audio: get the microphone close

Clear sound matters as much as a clear picture, and it is harder to fix later. Move the microphone close enough for clear speech, but listen for popping, clothing rustle and distortion.

- **Choose the quietest room you can.** Curtains, rugs and soft furniture all help reduce echo.
- **Remove background noise.** Close windows, turn off fans and silence notifications, including on the filming phone.
- **Record a test and listen on headphones.** You will catch hum or echo that you miss while talking.
- **Record a few seconds of silence** at the start of each session. That room tone can help in the edit if you need to cover a gap.

## 5. Takes: give yourself useful footage

- **Do a test take first.** Record thirty seconds and watch it back before filming the real thing.
- **Leave a beat before and after you speak.** A second or two of stillness gives you clean edit points.
- **Film in sections.** For longer tutorials, recording one section at a time keeps your energy up and makes mistakes cheaper to redo.
- **Say the take name aloud.** "Video two, section three, take one" makes clips much easier to find later.
- **Record key lines twice.** If a line really matters, get a spare while you are still set up.

## 6. Review: check before you pack away

Check your test take carefully, then do a quicker check at the end of the session. Look for:

- Focus on your eyes, not the background.
- Framing that has not drifted.
- Consistent light and colour across takes.
- Clear audio with no distortion, hum or echo.

Note your best takes while they are fresh. Then write down one thing to change next time. That small habit is how a setup improves without buying more kit.

## Your repeatable checklist

1. Write your one-sentence message and choose the format.
2. Rebuild the frame from your tape marks and photo.
3. Face the light, then lock focus and exposure if you can.
4. Quieten the room, then test and listen to the audio.
5. Do a test take, then film in sections with a beat at each end.
6. Review, note your best takes and one improvement.
