# Rollside blog: 12-week editorial plan

Planned 22 October 2026 for Mondays from 26 October 2026 to 11 January 2027 (Europe/Lisbon). One article per Lisbon calendar week. The machine-readable copy is [12-week-plan.json](12-week-plan.json).

## Overview

**What this plan is for.** Twelve complete, useful articles for solo creators who talk to camera, each answering one reader question that the existing four posts do not. The plan sets direction; it does not pre-write anything. Each author still researches, writes, edits and verifies on the day.

**Sequencing.** No content pillar runs two weeks in a row. Script craft opens (hooks), then delivery (filler words), a specific solo-filming problem (back camera), tutorials, script craft again (how much to script), and the first product-education post (remotes) lands in week six, once there is a run of non-product posts to link from. December carries a light seasonal angle twice (rehearsing a talk, then batch planning for January) without depending on it. Voice follow is explained in early January, and the last week covers editing, which the Mac editor may or may not be released for by then. That post is written to work either way.

**Distinct reader tasks.** The twelve posts cover at least ten different jobs: writing an opening, cutting filler words, framing without a screen, scripting a step-by-step, deciding how much to script, setting up a remote, rehearsing a live talk, preparing captions, adding vocal variety, planning a shoot day, choosing a scroll mode and editing takes.

**Avoiding keyword overlap.** Each post has a unique slug and primary keyword, and none targets the four existing intents: writing a teleprompter script, reading a teleprompter naturally, general talking-head setup, or words for a 60-second script. Where topics sit close together, the brief says how they differ:

- The hook post decides what the opening is; the 60-second post trims for length.
- The filler-words post is about unscripted speech; the monotone post is about pitch, stress and pace; the reading-naturally post is about eyeline and the prompter itself.
- The back-camera post is one framing problem; the setup post covers light, sound and the whole shot.
- The tutorial post is about sequence and visuals; the writing post is about sounding like yourself.
- The remote post is about hardware control; the voice-activated post is about scroll modes.
- The batch-filming post plans a session; the editing post deals with what comes out of it.

Do not add a second post later for any of these primary keywords. Update the existing article instead.

**Editing standard.** UK English. Every post needs a specific, clearly labelled hypothetical worked example and practical steps a reader can use the same day. Vary sentence length and structure, and vary the shape of the posts themselves (annotated transcript, comparison, rehearsal schedule, decision list, take log). No formulaic AI openings, throat-clearing or summary paragraphs that repeat the post. Use keywords where they read naturally, never forced. No em dashes. No unsupported statistics: cite a number only from a source actually read, or frame it as a planning estimate and show the inputs. Never invent lived experience, customers, tests, testimonials or results, and never claim human authorship or promise detector scores or SEO outcomes. Run a separate editorial pass before tests and build.

**Product claims.** As of 22 October 2026, Rollside Prompter is available on iPhone and iPad (version 1.2.2), Rollside for Mac is in preparation for direct release with checkout closed, and Rollside Camera and the Creator Bundle are in development with no public download. **Authors must recheck current product availability and every listed claim on live Rollside pages before each post.** Never write a tutorial for an unreleased app. CTAs stay light and truthful.

- **Slug:** `how-to-write-a-video-hook`

**Angle and difference.** Hooks for a face and a voice only. Differs from the 60-second script post, which trims an opening for length; this post decides what the opening should be in the first place. **Outline**

**How to stop saying um on camera without sounding scripted**

- **Slug:** `how-to-stop-saying-um-on-camera`
- **Primary keyword:** how to stop saying um on camera
- **Related terms:** filler words; verbal fillers in videos; pausing instead of um
- **Pillar:** Delivery and rehearsal
- **Search intent and reader question:** Problem-solving. Reader question: I watch my footage back and hear um, so, like everywhere. How do I cut them down without reading every word?

**Angle and difference.** Treats fillers as a planning problem, not a willpower problem: most appear at transitions and openings, so plan those moments. Accepts that some fillers are normal and that editing can remove a few. Differs from the reading-naturally post, which is about prompter eyeline and expression, not unscripted speech.

**Outline**

1. Find your pattern: record two minutes unscripted and mark where the fillers cluster
2. Plan the joins: write out openings and transitions, leave the middle in bullets, and scroll bullet sections manually or on a slow timed speed
3. Swap the filler for a pause and practise the silence
4. Slow the start, and decide which fillers can stay or be cut in the edit

**Worked example brief.** Hypothetical 20-second transcript of someone explaining how to choose a yoga mat, with each filler bolded and a note on where it falls (start, between points, before a number). Then the same passage with planned transition lines and marked pauses. State clearly that the transcript is invented for illustration and that no counts are measured results.

**Internal links.** [/blog/how-to-read-a-teleprompter-naturally/](https://rollside.studio/blog/how-to-read-a-teleprompter-naturally/), [/blog/how-to-write-a-teleprompter-script/](https://rollside.studio/blog/how-to-write-a-teleprompter-script/), [/blog/talking-head-video-setup/](https://rollside.studio/blog/talking-head-video-setup/)

**Writing and format.** Use an annotated transcript as the centrepiece. Avoid the cliché that fillers 'destroy credibility'. If citing advice that a speech-language therapist may help for non-habit causes, keep it brief and non-diagnostic.

**Product CTA.** Light: a script with written transitions and bullet notes can sit in Prompter. For bullet notes you talk around, recommend timed scrolling at a slow speed or moving the script by hand; do not suggest Voice follow will track improvised speech from sparse bullets.
